This claim is provocative and almost certainly overstated as written. But the underlying point is serious: water is 98% of your brewed coffee. The minerals dissolved in it are not neutral — they actively participate in extraction, binding to different flavor compounds and carrying them into the cup at different rates.

## What you're optimizing for

Specialty coffee extraction targets a dissolved solids range of roughly 1.15–1.35% by weight (the TDS, or total dissolved solids), with an extraction yield of 18–22% of the coffee's mass. Hit this window and you're extracting the majority of solubles without over-extracting the bitter, harsh compounds that dominate the late extraction.

Water chemistry affects your ability to hit this window consistently.

## Magnesium vs. calcium

Both magnesium and calcium bind to flavor compounds, but they bind differently. Magnesium ions are particularly effective at extracting the aromatic compounds responsible for brightness and complexity. Calcium tends to extract heavier, more neutral compounds. Water with higher magnesium content often produces brighter, more vibrant cups.

Most municipal water is higher in calcium. Specialty water recipes — and some commercial filter systems — adjust this balance.

## Practical implications

You don't need to become a water chemist. Two practical steps:
1. If your tap water is very hard (>200 ppm total hardness), filtered or bottled water will make a noticeable difference.
2. If you're already using filtered water and want to go further, Barista Hustle Water and a few similar products offer pre-formulated mineral packets.

Beyond that: grind freshness and grind consistency will return more improvement per effort than water chemistry. Learn the order of operations.
