A quartz watch keeps better time. It costs less, needs less servicing, and will outlast any mechanical movement in pure timekeeping accuracy. This is not a close call. A cheap quartz movement drifts a few seconds per month; a chronometer-certified mechanical movement can drift a few seconds per day.

And yet.

## What mechanical horology is actually about

The mainspring in a mechanical watch stores energy from winding and releases it through a series of gears and levers at a rate controlled by the escapement. The balance wheel oscillates — typically at 6, 8, or 10 beats per second — dividing time into countable increments.

This is a machine that runs on nothing but wound metal. No battery, no electronic oscillator, no software. It will run for decades with periodic cleaning and oil replacement. The mechanism is visible through a display caseback. You can watch it work.

That visibility is part of what distinguishes mechanical watchmaking from almost every other precision engineering field. The movement is the product. It is designed to be seen.

## Craft as signal

Wearing a mechanical watch is an unusual act in the modern world: choosing a less functional object because of how it was made. This is the same logic that buys hand-thrown pottery, hand-sewn shoes, or a hand-wound book. The value is in the human time encoded in the object.

A grand complication might represent ten thousand hours of a master watchmaker's work. The price reflects that. So does the object — if you know how to read it.
