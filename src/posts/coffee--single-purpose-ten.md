I want to make an argument for the undivided coffee break.

Not the cup consumed while reading email. Not the commute coffee. Not the poured-and-forgotten mug that goes cold on the desk. The cup that gets the full ten minutes, with nowhere to be and nothing open on the screen.

## What you notice

When you sit with just the cup — no podcast, no article, no scroll — a few things happen. You notice temperature more acutely: the shift from too-hot to drink to the ideal window to the cooling that signals time is running out. You notice the flavors as they change across that arc. A coffee that tastes like chocolate at 65°C might taste like dried fruit at 50°C.

You also notice your own mind, which is less comfortable. The instinct to open a browser or pick up a phone doesn't disappear just because you've decided not to. It surfaces, and you watch it surface, and then you drink more coffee.

## The case for one undistracted thing

There's a category of pleasures that require attention to be pleasures at all. A good film watched while multitasking is less than a mediocre film watched fully. A great meal eaten quickly and distracted is worse than a simple meal eaten slowly.

Coffee is in this category. It's designed — by plant biology, by processing, by the roaster, by whoever brewed it — to deliver flavor across time and temperature. To miss that arc is to have consumed something, but not to have tasted it.

Ten minutes. One cup. Nothing else. It's not a life philosophy. It's just a better way to drink coffee.
